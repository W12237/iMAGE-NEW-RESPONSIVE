# Madinet Masr reference study

Studied live on 4 October 2026 in English and Arabic, including the homepage, navigation layer, responsive structure, and representative project/news content. This document records transferable experience principles only.

## Visual principles

- Treat photography as the primary interface: wide, high-impact frames carry the story while UI remains light.
- Alternate cinematic, edge-to-edge scenes with bright editorial pauses. The contrast creates rhythm without decorative noise.
- Use a restrained palette and one strong accent; reserve the accent for active states, controls, and small moments of emphasis.
- Let scale, whitespace, crop, and sequencing create luxury rather than glass effects or dense card systems.

## Hero and opening

- A short, brand-led loading pause precedes a full-viewport hero.
- The hero is a single immersive media plane with a darkened image, minimal copy, one small action, and a discrete scroll cue.
- Header controls float over the media rather than occupying a separate bar.
- Motion is atmospheric: media scale, soft fades, and staged text entry rather than constant animation.

## Header and menu

- The closed header is exceptionally light: logo, language, utility action, and a circular menu trigger.
- The menu arrives as a second visual layer: a large pale panel with generous margins, numbered oversized links, a preview image, secondary actions, and a branded close control.
- Navigation hierarchy is created mainly with type scale, opacity, and spatial grouping.
- The menu feels modal and immersive, but retains an obvious escape route and a visible connection to the page beneath.

## Typography and section rhythm

- Display headings are large but not heavy; smaller eyebrow copy sets context.
- Sections tend to occupy a substantial part of the viewport, with relatively few elements inside each composition.
- The page deliberately changes pace: hero, bright editorial/project frame, dark/full-bleed media, then spacious text-led content.
- Body copy is narrow and concise. Whitespace is used as an active compositional element.

## Project presentation

- Projects are presented as one dominant visual stage, not a repeated grid.
- Classification and navigation sit around the frame; metadata is consolidated into a single information surface.
- The active project gets strong photography, location and product information, with the next/previous affordance treated as part of the composition.
- This supports browsing by mood first and facts second.

## Scrolling and motion

- Scrolling remains native and legible; transitions are tied to section entry rather than aggressive scroll hijacking.
- Full-viewport media sections create natural chapter breaks.
- Reveals use opacity, translation, crop, and scale. The most animated moments are the opening, menu, and project stage.
- Quiet sections remain quiet; not every text block animates independently.

## Desktop/mobile and EN/AR observations

- Desktop uses long horizontal alignments and generous margins, while mobile collapses those relationships into vertical editorial stacks.
- Large media remains dominant on mobile, but controls and metadata move below or overlay the lower portion of the image to preserve tap targets.
- The menu becomes a true full-screen mobile composition with simpler hierarchy and fewer simultaneous elements.
- Arabic uses native RTL flow and Arabic copy rather than a mechanically mirrored English layer. Directional controls, reading order, alignments, and image focal points need intentional reversal.
- Arabic display type needs more line height and generally a slightly smaller scale than Latin display type at the same breakpoint.

## What Image Developments should adapt

- A seamless hero/header relationship.
- A branded, immersive navigation layer.
- A single-stage project discovery sequence.
- Strong alternation between full-bleed media and calm editorial space.
- Sparse, purposeful motion with reduced-motion parity.
- Locale-aware composition, not translation alone.

## What we should not copy

- Brand marks, red accent, wording, project names, photos, exact navigation taxonomy, or exact screen compositions.
- The reference's rounded white project information panel or its precise carousel arrangement.
- Its exact loader, menu geometry, animation timings, and transition order.
- The dense amount of development taxonomy in the footer.

Image Developments should feel related in confidence and pacing, while using its own forest/cream/peach identity, more architectural typography, a quieter editorial tone, and a distinct vertical project narrative.
